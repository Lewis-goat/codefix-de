Breville / Sage Oracle Touch ER11: temperature sensor short circuit

ER11 bedeutet: Group head temperature sensor short circuit at startup.

Kurzantwort. The group head temperature sensor (NTC) is reading as a short circuit: the board sees near-zero resistance, which a healthy sensor never produces. The two real-world causes are water bridging the sensor contacts (a leak or steam at the probe seal) and a failed sensor. Because it failed at startup, it is usually a wet connector or a sensor that has already died.

Wann der Fehler auftrittbeim Einschalt-Selbsttest

ER11 beheben

  1. Switch off at the wall and unplug. Leave the machine for an hour so any moisture in the connectors dries, then try again. A short that clears after drying tells you there is a leak to find.
  2. Open the machine (base or rear panel) and inspect the group head temperature sensor (NTC) plug and the wiring near the boiler. Look for a wet patch, white scale trails or a perished o-ring around the probe.
  3. Replace any leaking o-rings at the boiler fittings and NTC probes. Kits are cheap and the leak, not the sensor, is often the true fault.
  4. If the code persists after everything is dry, replace the group head temperature sensor (NTC).
  5. Clear the error log and run the machine through a full heat-up and a shot to confirm.

Wann aufhören und zum Reparateur gehen

If you see scorch marks on the triac board or the machine trips your GFCI / RCD, stop: that is an electrical fault, not just a sensor.

Was es kostet

Ersatzteile: Genuine NTC sensor assemblies run about $25 to $95 depending on which sensor it is (steam wand and milk-jug sensors are the dear ones). O-ring kits are $10 to $20.

Service: Der Herstellerservice für einen Vollautomaten liegt außerhalb der Garantie typisch bei 250–500 € inkl. Rückversand; freie Espressowerkstätten sind bei einem einzelnen Teil meist günstiger.

Reparatur lohnend? Yes in most cases. Sensor plus o-ring kit is well under $100. If a leak has soaked the control board, get a quote before spending anything.

Fehlerprotokoll abrufen

Switch the machine off at the wall. Hold the front Power button, switch the wall power back on and release when the logo appears. Enter the service password 00000, then open Error Counter (stored faults) or Live Debug (live temperatures and water levels).

Löschen Sie das Protokoll nach der Reparatur, um zu prüfen, ob der Defekt wirklich weg ist.
